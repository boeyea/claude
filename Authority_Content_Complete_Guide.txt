You need to be producing content within 7 days of reading this.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CAMERA (Pick ONE):
- Your smartphone (iPhone 13+ or Samsung S22+)
- OR: Sony ZV-E10 / Canon EOS M50

That's it. No backup camera. No second body.

AUDIO (Pick ONE):

LIGHTING (Pick ONE setup):
- One softbox or LED panel, camera left, 45 degrees
- One reflector (or white foam board) opposite side

That's your only lighting setup for 90 days.
Master one thing before adding complexity.

STABILIZATION:
- Basic tripod ($50-100)
- No gimbal needed yet

TOTAL STARTUP BUDGET: $200-800 depending on camera choice

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.2 SET — ONE LOCATION ONLY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Pick ONE room. This is your studio for 90 days.

REQUIREMENTS:
- Can control light (curtains or no windows)
- Reasonably quiet
- Has a bed OR chair/couch
- Wall or background isn't embarrassing

PREPARATION (one-time, 2 hours max):
- Remove personal items from frame area
- Get solid-color bedding if using bed (gray, white, or dark)
- Clear one wall or corner completely
- Check phone/camera angle — what's visible?

DO NOT:
- Repaint
- Buy furniture
- Create multiple "sets"
- Spend more than $200 on set dressing

Good enough is the goal.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

HER (3 outfits total):

1. AUTHORITY LOOK
   - Black blazer + white/cream top

   - This is your thumbnail uniform

2. INTIMATE LOOK
   - One quality lingerie set (black or deep color)
   - OR silk robe
   - Not both needed yet

3. CASUAL LOOK
   - Fitted loungewear
   - Leggings + simple top
   - "At home" vibe

That's it. Rotate these. Don't buy more until day 60.

HIM (2 outfits total):

1. DRESSED
   - Button-down shirt (white or light blue)
   - Dark jeans or chinos

2. MINIMAL
   - Fitted plain t-shirt
   - Jeans or joggers

He's not the visual focus. Don't overthink his wardrobe.

================================================================================
                    CHAPTER 2: THE ONLY RULES THAT MATTER
                             (Non-Negotiables)
================================================================================

Forget elaborate rule systems.
Here are the only ones that matter for launch:

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 FIVE VISUAL RULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. SHE IS HIGHER OR CENTERED IN FRAME
   - Always. No exceptions.
   - If both in shot, she takes visual priority.

2. CLEAN BACKGROUND
   - Nothing embarrassing or distracting visible.
   - Check every frame before posting.

3. ADEQUATE LIGHTING
   - Face is visible and not shadowed badly.
   - "Good enough" standard, not cinematic.

4. STABLE FOOTAGE
   - Use tripod. No handheld.
   - Shaky = amateur = scroll past.

5. SHE LOOKS COMFORTABLE, NOT TRYING
   - Relaxed posture.
   - Not performing "sexy."
   - Calm > eager.

That's it. Follow these five. Ignore everything else for now.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.2 THREE BEHAVIORAL RULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. SHE MOVES FIRST, HE RESPONDS
   - In any two-shot, she initiates.
   - He waits, then reacts.

2. LESS TALKING
   - Minimal dialogue.
   - Silence is fine.
   - Don't explain the dynamic.

3. SLOW DOWN
   - Whatever pace feels natural, go 30% slower.
   - You will feel like it's too slow. It's not.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.3 TWO POSTING RULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. CAPTIONS: 5 WORDS OR LESS
   - Or no caption at all.
   - Never explain what's happening.

2. POST BEFORE YOU'RE READY
   - If it meets minimum quality, post it.
   - Perfectionism is the enemy right now.

================================================================================
                    CHAPTER 3: CONTENT PRODUCTION RHYTHM
                           (Sustainable Output)
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 WEEKLY TARGETS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

MINIMUM VIABLE OUTPUT:
- 4-5 feed posts per week
- 1 PPV piece every 1-2 weeks

This is the floor, not the ceiling.

SHOOTING SCHEDULE:
- One shoot session per week (2-3 hours)
- Batch content: create 5-7 pieces per session
- Don't shoot daily — it leads to burnout

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.2 THE "GOOD ENOUGH" STANDARD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Before posting, ask only:
1. Is she visible and well-lit? (Yes/No)
2. Is background acceptable? (Yes/No)
3. Is it stable? (Yes/No)
4. Does it fit the basic authority vibe? (Yes/No)

4 yes answers = post it.

DO NOT ASK:
- Is it perfect?
- Is the lighting cinematic?
- Does it match my ideal brand vision?
- What will critics think?

Those questions kill output.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.3 BURNOUT DAYS — FALLBACK CONTENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

You will have days where you can't perform, can't shoot, can't create.

FALLBACK OPTIONS (no shame, use them):

1. STILL IMAGE, NO FACE
   - Cropped body shot
   - Detail shot (hands, clothing, environment)
   - Caption: "..." or nothing

2. RECYCLED CONTENT
   - Re-edit old footage
   - Different crop of previous photo
   - Alternate take you didn't use

3. TEXT POST
   - Rare, but acceptable
   - "Thinking." or "Soon."
   - Maintains presence without production

4. BEHIND-THE-SCENES
   - Setting up (no face needed)
   - Equipment shot
   - "Preparing."

The goal is PRESENCE, not perfection.
An 80% post beats no post.

================================================================================
                    CHAPTER 4: THE FEEDBACK LOOP
                        (Critical For Success)
================================================================================

This is the most important chapter.
Without this, you're guessing forever.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 WEEKLY METRICS REVIEW (Every Sunday — 30 minutes)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Track these numbers weekly:

GROWTH:
- Subscribers gained
- Subscribers lost
- Net change

ENGAGEMENT:
- Likes per post (average)
- Comments per post (average)
- Messages received

REVENUE:
- PPV purchases (count)
- PPV revenue ($)
- Tips received

Write these down. Compare week over week.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.2 WHAT TO LOOK FOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WORKING (do more):
- Posts with 2x average likes
- PPV that sells within 24 hours
- Comments that mention specific elements

NOT WORKING (do less):
- Posts with 50% below average engagement
- PPV that doesn't sell in first week
- Crickets — no response at all

PATTERNS TO NOTICE:
- Does lingerie outperform casual?
- Do solo shots beat two-shots?
- Do morning posts beat evening?
- Does minimal caption beat longer?
- Do longer videos outperform short clips?

You don't know until you measure.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.3 A/B TESTING — SIMPLE VERSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Each week, test ONE variable:

Week 1: Post times
- Half posts morning, half evening
- Which gets more engagement?

Week 2: Caption style
- Half with caption, half without
- Which performs better?

Week 3: Content type
- More solo vs more duo content
- Which gets response?

Week 4: Outfit
- Authority look vs intimate look
- Which drives more PPV?

Week 5: Video length
- Short clips vs longer scenes
- Which converts better?

ONE VARIABLE AT A TIME.
Otherwise you learn nothing.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.4 KILL RULES — WHEN TO STOP SOMETHING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

KILL an approach if:
- 3 consecutive posts underperform average by 50%+
- PPV doesn't sell a single copy in 7 days
- You dread doing it (burnout signal)

PIVOT when:
- Something unexpectedly overperforms (do more)
- Audience asks for something specific (consider it)
- Platform changes rules (adapt fast)

Don't kill things too early (give 2-3 weeks minimum).
Don't keep things too long (3 weeks of failure = stop).

================================================================================
                    CHAPTER 6: PLATFORM REALITY
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.1 ALGORITHM TRUTH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Platforms reward:
- Consistency (posting regularly)
- Engagement velocity (likes/comments in first hour)
- Retention (subscribers staying)
- Momentum (growth trend)

Platforms don't reward:
- Quality alone
- Perfectionism
- Sporadic "amazing" content
- Purity of vision

A mediocre post today beats a perfect post next week.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.2 SHADOW LIMITS AND MODERATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

ASSUME:
- Your content may be throttled without notice
- Rules change without warning
- Appeals are slow and often fail
- Backup everything externally

PROTECT YOURSELF:
- Never put all content on one platform
- Keep copies of everything
- Build email list or off-platform presence
- Don't rely on any single account

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.3 CONTROLLED IMPERFECTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is counterintuitive but important:

TOO POLISHED content can:
- Feel corporate/cold
- Intimidate potential subscribers
- Seem "unattainable"
- Reduce parasocial connection

STRATEGIC IMPERFECTION:
- Occasional "casual" posts
- Behind-the-scenes moments
- Slightly less produced content
- Human moments

This builds connection.
Don't mistake authenticity for sloppiness, but don't sterilize everything.

7.1 EMOTIONAL REALITY OF EARLY STAGE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

YOU WILL FEEL:
- Exposed and vulnerable

If you're creating with a partner:

SEPARATE performance from relationship
- On-camera dynamic ≠ real relationship
- Check in about feelings regularly

HAVE A QUIT PLAN
- Agree on conditions that would stop this
- No shame in stopping if it damages the relationship
- The relationship matters more than the content

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.3 HANDLING NO RESPONSE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Early on, you'll post and hear nothing.
- No likes
- No comments
- No messages
- No sales

THIS DOES NOT MEAN:
- Your content is bad
- Nobody wants this
- You should change everything
- You should quit

IT MEANS:
- You're new and have no audience yet
- The algorithm hasn't found your people
- Building takes time

RESPONSE:
- Keep posting
- Gather data
- Don't react emotionally to any single post
- Evaluate in 2-week windows, not daily

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.4 HANDLING NEGATIVE FEEDBACK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

You will eventually get:
- Mean comments
- Rejection
- Comparison insults
- Trolls

RESPONSE:
- Block without engaging
- Delete without responding
- Never defend yourself publicly
- Never apologize publicly

Your silence is your power.
Engagement (even negative) feeds trolls.

                    CHAPTER 9: CONTENT FORMAT OVERVIEW
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9.1 THE CONTENT HIERARCHY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Your content exists in layers. Each serves a different purpose.

LAYER 1: FEED CONTENT (Daily presence)
Purpose: Keep subscribers engaged, maintain visibility
Format: Photos, short clips (15-60 seconds)
Frequency: 4-5 per week minimum
Revenue: Indirect (keeps subscribers paying monthly)

LAYER 2: STANDARD PPV (Regular monetization)
Purpose: Direct revenue, reward engaged subscribers
Format: 3-10 minute videos
Frequency: 1-2 per week
Revenue: $15-40 per piece

LAYER 3: PREMIUM LONG-FORM (High-value releases)
Purpose: Major revenue events, catalog building
Format: 15-45 minute videos
Frequency: 2-4 per month
Revenue: $50-150 per piece

LAYER 4: CUSTOM CONTENT (On request)
Purpose: Premium pricing, subscriber loyalty
Format: Varies by request
Frequency: As requested
Revenue: $100-500+ depending on complexity

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9.2 THE CASE FOR LONG-FORM VIDEO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

You mentioned thinking about long videos. Here's the reality:

WHY LONG-FORM WORKS FOR THIS NICHE:

1. Authority requires patience
   - Quick clips feel rushed
   - Slow pacing needs room to breathe
   - The fantasy unfolds over time, not in 30 seconds

2. Higher perceived value
   - 5-minute video = $20
   - 30-minute video = $75-100
   - Price per minute actually increases with length

3. Differentiation
   - Most creators post quick clips
   - Long-form is rarer, more premium
   - Builds reputation for quality

4. Better subscriber retention
   - Subscribers who buy long-form are invested
   - Creates "event" content they anticipate
   - Builds catalog value over time

5. Matches the aesthetic
   - Stillness and slow pacing feel natural in longer content
   - Short clips force rushed energy
   - Your niche benefits from extended tension

WHY LONG-FORM IS HARDER:

1. Production overhead
   - More planning required
   - Longer shoot sessions
   - More editing time

2. Performance endurance
   - Maintaining presence for 30+ minutes
   - Staying in character
   - Managing energy

3. Technical challenges
   - Storage space
   - Continuous power/battery
   - Consistent lighting over time
   - Audio for extended takes

4. Higher risk per piece
   - More time invested
   - If it doesn't sell, bigger loss
   - Harder to pivot mid-production

VERDICT:
Long-form is excellent for this niche, but don't start with it.
Build to it after proving the concept works.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9.3 CONTENT FORMAT DECISION MATRIX
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHEN TO USE EACH FORMAT:

PHOTOS:
- Daily feed presence
- Thumbnail/preview images
- Quick production days
- Testing new looks/outfits
- Behind-the-scenes

SHORT CLIPS (15-60 seconds):
- Feed content
- Teasers for longer content
- Testing new concepts
- Story posts
- Quick moments/reactions

STANDARD VIDEO (3-10 minutes):
- Regular PPV releases
- "Complete" shorter scenes
- Focused single-concept content
- Building toward longer content
- Testing pacing/structure

LONG-FORM VIDEO (15-45 minutes):
- Premium PPV releases
- Full scene development
- "Event" content
- Catalog centerpieces
- Subscriber loyalty rewards

VERY LONG-FORM (45+ minutes):
- Only after proven demand
- Special releases
- Possibly serialized content
- Requires established audience

================================================================================
                    CHAPTER 10: LONG-FORM VIDEO PRODUCTION
                         (Your Primary Interest)
================================================================================

Since you're thinking about long videos, here's a complete breakdown.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10.1 WHAT "LONG-FORM" MEANS IN THIS CONTEXT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

LENGTH DEFINITIONS:

Standard long: 15-25 minutes
Premium long: 25-40 minutes
Extended: 40-60 minutes
Feature: 60+ minutes (rare, advanced)

FOR YOUR NICHE, THE SWEET SPOT IS: 20-35 minutes

Why:
- Long enough for proper pacing
- Short enough to maintain quality
- Fits the slow, deliberate aesthetic
- High perceived value without being overwhelming
- Manageable production and editing

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10.2 LONG-FORM STRUCTURE FOR AUTHORITY CONTENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The key to long-form in this niche: IT'S NOT ABOUT FILLING TIME.
It's about letting moments breathe.

STRUCTURAL FRAMEWORK (25-35 minute video):

ACT ONE: ESTABLISHMENT (5-8 minutes)
- She is present, occupied with something
- Environment is shown (her space)
- He enters or is already waiting
- Power dynamic is SHOWN, not told
- Minimal or no dialogue
- Tension through distance and stillness

What this looks like:
- Opening shot: her, alone, doing something (reading, makeup, looking out window)
- Hold for 30-60 seconds
- He appears in frame or she acknowledges his presence
- She continues what she was doing
- He waits
- Eventually, she gives him attention — brief
- The pace is established: she decides

ACT TWO: DEVELOPMENT (12-18 minutes)
- Gradual proximity
- She directs (physically or minimally verbally)
- He responds
- Power dynamics play out through action
- Intensity builds slowly
- Multiple "beats" or moments

Key principle: LESS IS MORE
- Long takes
- Silence between actions
- Let the camera hold on stillness
- Every escalation is earned

What this looks like:
- Movement toward each other is gradual
- She controls pace of any escalation
- He responds, never initiates
- Pauses are frequent and intentional
- If there's dialogue, it's sparse and commanding
- Physical interaction follows clear hierarchy

ACT THREE: RESOLUTION (5-10 minutes)
- Climax of the dynamic (however explicit your content is)
- She remains composed
- He remains attentive/responsive
- Clean ending — no messy wrap-up

ENDING OPTIONS:
- Fade to black after natural conclusion
- Return to composed stillness (she adjusts clothing, dismisses him)
- Brief moment of her alone, composed
- NO talking to camera
- NO "thank you for watching"
- NO breaking character

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10.3 PACING FOR LONG-FORM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THE BIGGEST MISTAKE: Trying to fill time with action.

CORRECT APPROACH: Let stillness carry the content.

SHOT DURATION GUIDELINES (Long-form):

Establishing shots: 30-90 seconds
Wide shots during action: 20-45 seconds
Medium shots: 15-30 seconds
Close-ups: 10-20 seconds
Reaction shots: 8-15 seconds

COMPARE TO SHORT CLIPS:
- Short clips: everything happens in 30-60 seconds
- Long-form: the first 5 minutes might have almost nothing happen

THIS IS THE POINT.

The authority niche sells patience, composure, and control.
Long-form content should FEEL unhurried.

If viewers feel rushed, you've failed.
If viewers feel hypnotized by the pacing, you've succeeded.

PRACTICAL PACING TECHNIQUES:

1. DELAYED RESPONSE
   - She speaks or gestures
   - Beat of silence (2-3 seconds)
   - He responds
   - Creates tension, shows hierarchy

2. HELD GAZE
   - Eye contact between them
   - Hold longer than feels natural (5-8 seconds)
   - She looks away first (on her terms)
   - Creates intensity without action

3. SLOW PHYSICAL MOVEMENT
   - Any movement happens at 50% normal speed
   - Walking, turning, reaching
   - Conveys deliberation and control

4. SILENCE AS TOOL
   - Entire minutes can pass with minimal sound
   - Room tone, breathing, fabric
   - Dialogue is earned, not filling

5. REPEATED STILLNESS
   - Return to static shots between action
   - Reestablishes calm
   - Prevents content from feeling rushed

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10.4 TECHNICAL REQUIREMENTS FOR LONG-FORM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Long videos have specific technical needs:

CAMERA:
- Must shoot continuously for 30-60 minutes
- Check record limits (some cameras stop at 30 min)
- Overheating can be an issue
- Recommended: Cameras with unlimited record time
  * Panasonic GH5/GH6
  * Sony A7C / A7 IV
  * Canon R5/R6 (with cooling fan or ambient temp control)

STORAGE:
- 4K 30 minutes = approximately 15-25 GB
- Bring multiple cards or one large card (256GB+)
- V60 or V90 cards for 4K

POWER:
- Camera batteries may not last full shoot
- Use dummy battery + AC adapter OR
- Multiple batteries with quick swap plan
- Never lose a take to dead battery

AUDIO:
- Wireless lav batteries should last 4+ hours
- Have backup batteries anyway
- Check levels before AND during long takes
- Record backup audio if possible

LIGHTING:
- LED panels with AC power (not battery)
- Consistent color temperature throughout
- Don't rely on window light (changes over 30 minutes)
- Test for 30 minutes before shoot to check for flicker

MEMORY:
- Know your shots before starting
- Have a loose structure (see 10.2)
- Don't try to wing a 30-minute scene
- But don't over-script either

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10.5 LONG-FORM SHOOTING STRATEGY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

APPROACH A: SINGLE CONTINUOUS TAKE

What it is:
- Record entire scene as one long take
- Minimal cuts in final edit
- Real-time pacing

Advantages:
- Authentic, immersive feel
- Easier editing
- Natural pacing
- Less technical complexity

Disadvantages:
- One mistake can ruin the take
- Harder to maintain energy
- Less flexibility in edit
- Technical failures are catastrophic

Best for:
- Experienced performers
- Simple setups
- Intimate, dialogue-free content
- When you want "fly on the wall" feel

APPROACH B: SEGMENTED SHOOTING

What it is:
- Shoot in segments (5-10 minutes each)
- Edit together for continuous feel
- Planned structure with flexibility

Advantages:
- Mistakes can be isolated
- Energy can be managed
- More flexibility in edit
- Can reshoot segments if needed

Disadvantages:
- Continuity challenges
- More editing required
- Risk of inconsistent pacing
- Can feel "assembled"

Best for:
- Early long-form attempts
- Complex scenes
- When learning pacing
- Multiple outfit/position changes

APPROACH C: HYBRID

What it is:
- Core scenes shot continuously
- Establishing/transition shots separate
- Best of both approaches

How it works:
- Shoot opening sequence (her alone) separately
- Shoot main interaction in 1-2 long takes
- Shoot closing/fade out separately
- Edit together seamlessly

Advantages:
- Flexibility where needed
- Authenticity where it matters
- Manageable production

RECOMMENDATION FOR STARTING:
Use Approach B (segmented) for your first few long-form pieces.
Move to Approach C (hybrid) once comfortable.
Consider Approach A only for premium releases after significant experience.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10.6 LONG-FORM EDITING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PHILOSOPHY: Editing long-form is about REMOVAL, not addition.

You're cutting the unnecessary, not building complexity.

EDITING PRINCIPLES:

1. PRESERVE PACING
   - Don't speed up slow moments
   - Silence is intentional, not boring
   - Resist urge to "tighten"

2. MINIMAL CUTS
   - Fewer cuts = more immersive
   - Let shots breathe
   - Only cut when necessary (new angle, position change)

3. NO FLASHY TRANSITIONS
   - Cut or dissolve only
   - No effects, no zoom transitions
   - Premium content is understated

4. AUDIO CONSISTENCY
   - Room tone should be consistent
   - No sudden volume changes
   - Music (if any) should be subtle, barely noticed

5. COLOR GRADE ONCE
   - Same grade across entire piece
   - Warm, slightly desaturated
   - Don't grade scene-by-scene differently

EDITING WORKFLOW:

1. Import and backup immediately
2. Review all footage (note best takes)
3. Assemble rough cut in order
4. Remove obvious mistakes/dead spots
5. Fine-tune pacing (usually extending, not shortening)
6. Audio cleanup
7. Color grade
8. Export preview, watch full length
9. Adjust if needed
10. Final export

EXPORT SETTINGS FOR LONG-FORM:

Codec: H.264 or H.265
Resolution: 4K preferred, 1080p acceptable
Bitrate: 20-35 Mbps for 4K, 10-15 Mbps for 1080p
Audio: AAC 320kbps
Frame rate: 24fps (cinematic) or 30fps

File sizes:
- 25 min at 4K = approximately 4-6 GB
- 25 min at 1080p = approximately 2-3 GB

Platform upload limits vary. Check before exporting.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10.7 LONG-FORM PRICING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PRICING FRAMEWORK:

15-20 minutes: $40-60
20-30 minutes: $60-90
30-45 minutes: $90-125
45-60 minutes: $125-175
60+ minutes: $150-250

FACTORS THAT INCREASE PRICE:
- Higher production value
- Established audience/demand
- Exclusive content
- Limited availability
- Special themes or requests

FACTORS THAT DECREASE VALUE:
- Poor pacing (feels padded)
- Technical issues
- Inconsistent quality
- Too much available elsewhere

PRICING PSYCHOLOGY:

Price communicates value.
- Too low: "This must not be good"
- Too high: "Not worth it"
- Just right: "Premium but accessible"

For long-form, err toward HIGHER pricing.
People expect to pay more for longer content.
Low prices devalue the time investment.

TIERED RELEASE STRATEGY:

Week 1: Full price ($80)
Week 2-3: Still full price
Week 4: Consider slight discount for non-buyers ($65)
After 30 days: Add to catalog at standard price

Never discount immediately. It trains subscribers to wait.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10.8 BUILDING TO LONG-FORM (The Path)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Don't start with 45-minute productions.
Build to it systematically.

PHASE 1: DAYS 1-30
- Focus on short clips (30-90 seconds)
- Learn basic production
- Find your rhythm
- No long-form yet

PHASE 2: DAYS 31-60
- Introduce standard PPV (3-7 minutes)
- Practice sustained performance
- Test pacing in shorter format
- No long-form yet

PHASE 3: DAYS 61-90
- Extend to 7-12 minute pieces
- Start planning first long-form
- Build subscriber anticipation
- Tease upcoming "full scene"

PHASE 4: MONTH 4+
- First long-form release (15-20 minutes)
- Evaluate response
- Adjust based on feedback
- Build to longer pieces gradually

PHASE 5: MONTH 6+
- Regular long-form releases (1-2 per month)
- Extend to 25-35 minutes
- Build catalog
- Consider "series" or connected content

This progression prevents:
- Burnout from over-ambition
- Technical failures from inexperience
- Low sales from unproven audience
- Wasted effort on content nobody wants
